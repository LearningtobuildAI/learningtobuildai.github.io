PART 3Engineering & Build Guide

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Radio-frequency sensing front end — detects the tiny body movements caused by breathing and heartbeat, without any contact. Key parts: Wi-Fi radios with channel-state-information readout, or mmWave radar modules, antenna array, signal-capture firmware.
  • Thermal sensing — reads skin and breath temperature patterns, and confirms a person is present. Key parts: uncooled microbolometer or thermopile array, low-resolution IR lens, shutter/calibration.
  • Vital-sign extraction — turns noisy radio and thermal signals into respiration and heart rates. Key parts: clutter and motion filters, frequency-domain analysis, beamforming/localisation to find the subject, motion-artefact rejection.
  • Sensor fusion — combines the two modalities so that one covers the other's blind spots. Key parts: fusion model, confidence estimation, person tracking.
  • Edge compute and privacy layer — keeps raw data in the home and outputs only health parameters. Key parts: on-device processor, encryption, no-raw-image or raw-signal retention policy.
  • Alerts and care integration — escalates anomalies to the household or a carer. Key parts: trend history, threshold and anomaly alerts, app or telehealth interface.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Pick the room and sensor positions. Radio sensing needs the person within range of the antennas and a reasonably stable multipath environment; thermal needs line of sight to the face or chest.
  2. Baseline the empty room. The system records how radio waves bounce off walls and furniture with nobody present, so those echoes can be subtracted.
  3. Collect labelled data. Volunteers lie or sit in view while a reference method — a chest strap or ECG — records true respiration and heart rate, building a training set.
  4. Train the separation model. Breathing sits near 0.2–0.3 Hz and heartbeat near 1–1.5 Hz; the model must pull the weaker heartbeat signal out of chest movement, posture shifts and speech.
  5. Add thermal channels. Breath-related temperature changes near the nostrils give a second respiration estimate, and skin temperature adds a coarse thermal trend.
  6. Fuse and score confidence. The system reports a reading only when radio and thermal agree and motion is low; otherwise it flags the measurement as unusable.
  7. Validate against clinical reference. Accuracy is checked across age, body size, clothing and sleep position, which is what regulators require before health claims are made.
  8. Deploy with privacy by design. Only derived numbers leave the device; raw signals and thermal frames are processed and discarded locally.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Wi-Fi channel-state-information access on cheap chipsets — sensing works best where firmware exposes fine-grained channel data, which consumer hardware does not always allow.
  • EXISTS Low-cost uncooled thermal arrays — these already sit in consumer devices, though at low resolution and modest thermal sensitivity.
  • PARTIAL Machine-learning models robust to real rooms — laboratory accuracy falls sharply with movement, multiple people and unusual furniture layouts.
  • PARTIAL Clinical validation and medical-device clearance — wellness readings are easy to ship; trusted diagnosis requires evidence and regulation.
  • EXISTS On-device compute with strong privacy controls — contactless sensing of health in the home is only acceptable if raw data never leaves it.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: radio-based presence, sleep and fall detection in smart-home devices — contactless awareness without cameras or wearables.
  2. Next: wellness-grade respiration and heart-rate trends validated in the home — early warning of deterioration for older or chronically ill people.
  3. Next: regulatory clearance for specific clinical uses such as sleep apnoea screening — use in telehealth and remote care programmes.
  4. Longer term: multi-room, multi-person monitoring fused with clinical records — continuous passive care that reduces hospital admissions — provided accuracy, bias and privacy are shown to hold up.
