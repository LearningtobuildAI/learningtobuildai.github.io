9Engineering Breakdown
  • Microphone array — captures speech at high fidelity while suppressing background noise. Key parts: MEMS microphones, acoustic baffles, analog front-end.
  • Compute and wireless — processes audio locally and streams it to the cloud over a wireless link. Key parts: low-power system-on-chip, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi radio, antenna.
  • Power — keeps the pendant running for a full day of intermittent recording. Key parts: lithium-polymer battery, charging contacts, power management IC.
  • Enclosure and wearability — houses the electronics and attaches to clothing or a lanyard. Key parts: plastic or metal shell, clip or lanyard loop, status LED, button or touch surface.
  • Cloud AI service — transcribes speech, generates summaries and stores searchable memories. Key parts: speech-to-text models, large language models, vector database, API endpoints.
  • Privacy and security — gives the wearer control over what is recorded and shared. Key parts: encryption in transit and at rest, local wake-word detection, mute switch, data retention policy.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Design the electronics. Choose a low-power SoC, microphones, battery and radio, then lay out a compact printed circuit board.
  2. Write the firmware. Implement audio capture, Bluetooth pairing, power management and a local wake-word engine.
  3. Build the cloud pipeline. Set up speech-to-text, an LLM for summarisation, and a database that stores transcripts and embeddings.
  4. Assemble the hardware. Solder components, connect the battery, and fit everything into the enclosure with the microphone ports.
  5. Calibrate the microphones. Test audio capture in cafés, cars and windy streets, then tune beamforming and noise suppression.
  6. Test battery and connectivity. Verify all-day battery life under realistic use and confirm reliable pairing and cloud sync.
  7. Certify and manufacture. Pass electromagnetic, safety and privacy compliance tests, then start mass production.
  8. Deploy and update. Ship the device, push over-the-air firmware updates and improve the cloud models over time.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS MEMS microphones — tiny, low-power microphones make a pendant-sized recorder possible.
  • EXISTS Bluetooth Low Energy — provides a low-power link to a phone or direct cloud gateway.
  • EXISTS Cloud speech-to-text — turns recorded audio into searchable text.
  • EXISTS Large language models — generate summaries, reminders and answers from transcripts.
  • EXISTS Miniature lithium batteries — store enough energy for a day of use in a small, light package.
  • PARTIAL On-device AI accelerators — would allow local processing for privacy and offline use, but are still limited by power and cost.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: pendants that record conversations and produce cloud summaries — hands-free note-taking and memory capture for early adopters.
  2. Next: on-device speech and language processing — better privacy, lower latency and offline operation.
  3. Next: integration with AR glasses and other wearables — multimodal context from what the wearer sees and hears.
  4. Longer term: always-on personal assistant with proactive memory and action — a wearable that anticipates needs and acts across services.
