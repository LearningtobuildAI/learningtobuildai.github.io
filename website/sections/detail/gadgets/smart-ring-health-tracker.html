9Engineering Breakdown
  • Optical sensor module — measures blood volume changes in finger arteries using light. Key parts: green and infrared LEDs, photodiodes, optical barriers to reduce stray light.
  • Motion sensing — tracks hand movement and orientation for sleep staging and activity recognition. Key parts: 3-axis accelerometer, optional gyroscope.
  • Processing and memory — runs algorithms on the ring for heart rate, heart rate variability (HRV) and sleep stages. Key parts: low-power microcontroller, flash memory, algorithm firmware.
  • Power — provides several days of operation from a very small battery. Key parts: rechargeable lithium-polymer cell, power management integrated circuit, charging contacts or inductive coil.
  • Wireless and charging — syncs data to a phone and receives charge. Key parts: Bluetooth Low Energy radio, antenna, charging dock.
  • Mechanical housing — protects electronics and fits comfortably on a finger. Key parts: outer shell (titanium or plastic), inner molding, sizing inserts, water-resistant seal.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Design the sensor array. Place LEDs and photodiodes to get a strong signal from finger arteries while minimising motion artefacts and crosstalk.
  2. Fabricate the ring shell. Machine or mould the outer shell and inner structure from biocompatible, skin-safe materials, leaving precise openings for sensors and charging contacts.
  3. Assemble the electronics. Populate a flexible or rigid printed circuit board with the sensors, microcontroller, radio and battery, then test each board.
  4. Calibrate each unit. Individually calibrate the optical path and motion response so raw readings are comparable across rings.
  5. Develop firmware. Write algorithms for PPG signal processing, HRV calculation, sleep staging and aggressive power management to extend battery life.
  6. Seal and finish. Insert the electronics into the shell, seal the ring for water resistance, and attach sizing inserts.
  7. Validate performance. Test heart-rate accuracy against a chest strap or ECG, sleep staging against lab polysomnography, and battery life under typical use.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Miniaturised photoplethysmography (PPG) sensors — they measure blood volume changes from a finger, the basis for heart rate and HRV.
  • EXISTS Low-power microcontrollers — they run signal processing on the ring without draining the tiny battery.
  • EXISTS Bluetooth Low Energy — it syncs data to a phone with very little energy.
  • EXISTS High-density lithium-polymer batteries — they fit several days of power into a ring-sized cell.
  • PARTIAL Sleep-staging algorithms from HRV and motion — they work reasonably well for many people but are not yet as accurate as lab sleep studies for everyone.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: consumer rings that track sleep, heart rate and recovery — personal, continuous insight into rest and readiness.
  2. Next: improved accuracy and regulatory clearances for detecting irregular heart rhythms — early alerts for conditions such as atrial fibrillation.
  3. Next: integration with clinical remote-monitoring platforms — continuous monitoring for chronic heart conditions outside hospital.
  4. Longer term: non-invasive sensing of additional biomarkers such as blood pressure or glucose trends — broader preventive health management from a wearable.
