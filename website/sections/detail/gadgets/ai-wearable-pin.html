9Engineering Breakdown
  • Sensing — captures the user's voice, images and surroundings in real time. Key parts: camera module, microphone array, light and proximity sensors.
  • Compute — runs AI models on the device and decides what to send to the cloud. Key parts: system-on-chip with CPU, GPU and NPU, RAM and flash storage, thermal management.
  • Connectivity — links the wearable to a phone, Wi-Fi or cellular network for cloud AI and updates. Key parts: Bluetooth radio, Wi-Fi radio, optional cellular modem, antennas.
  • Power — supplies energy for always-ready operation and recharging. Key parts: lithium-polymer battery, power management IC, charging contacts or wireless coil.
  • AI software stack — turns raw audio and images into answers, translations and memories. Key parts: speech recognition, text-to-speech, vision models, large language model (on-device or cloud), personal memory and context store.
  • Mechanical housing — makes the device wearable and protects the electronics. Key parts: clip or pendant body, lens cover, buttons or touch surface, weather sealing.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Define the use case and form factor. Decide what the assistant must do hands-free — answering questions, remembering conversations, translating — and design a clip or pendant that can be worn all day.
  2. Select or design the system-on-chip. Choose a processor with a neural processing unit powerful enough for on-device speech and vision, while keeping power draw low enough for a small battery.
  3. Build the AI software stack. Train or fine-tune speech recognition, vision and language models; design the memory system that stores and retrieves personal context; implement privacy controls that decide what stays on the device.
  4. Integrate hardware and software. Port the models to the chip, tune them for latency and battery life, and write the firmware that manages sensors, connectivity and power states.
  5. Prototype and test. Build working units, test voice pickup in noisy places, camera performance in different light, battery endurance, and the accuracy and safety of AI responses.
  6. Manufacture and assemble. Produce circuit boards, mould the housing, assemble the camera, microphones, battery and chip, and seal the device against dust and water.
  7. Calibrate and validate. Calibrate microphones and camera, run automated tests, and obtain any required radio and safety certifications.
  8. Deploy and update. Ship the device with companion apps, then push over-the-air updates to improve models and fix issues, since the AI is the core of the product.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Low-power edge AI chips — neural processing units in mobile chips make on-device speech and vision feasible without draining the battery.
  • EXISTS Compact camera and microphone arrays — small, high-quality sensors are needed to capture usable images and isolate the user's voice.
  • EXISTS Large language models and speech recognition — they provide the conversational and understanding abilities that make the assistant useful.
  • EXISTS Wireless connectivity — Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and cellular links let the device offload heavy computation to the cloud.
  • PARTIAL High-density batteries — always-on cameras and AI are power-hungry, so battery life remains a major constraint.
  • PARTIAL Privacy-preserving on-device AI — users need assurance that constant audio and video capture does not leak sensitive data; running more models locally helps.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: clip-on and pendant assistants that answer voice queries and capture notes, with mixed on-device and cloud processing — hands-free access to AI in everyday situations.
  2. Next: on-device large language models that keep personal data local and reduce latency — more private, responsive assistance without a constant cloud connection.
  3. Next: real-time multimodal understanding — combining what the device sees and hears into contextual help — assistance for tasks like translation, navigation and object recognition.
  4. Longer term: ambient computing integrated with lightweight AR glasses and smart environments — an assistant that fades into the background and is always available when needed.
