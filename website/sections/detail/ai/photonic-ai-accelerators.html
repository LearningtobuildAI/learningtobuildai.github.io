PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

These chips utilize silicon-photonic meshes where optical signals carry out multiply-accumulate operations as light passes through tunable interferometers. Electronics are limited to the edges of these devices, reducing power consumption and increasing speed compared to purely electronic counterparts.

5Materials Used
  • silicon
  • tunable interferometers
  • optical fibers
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The manufacturing process involves creating silicon-based photonic components integrated with traditional electronics. This requires advanced semiconductor fabrication techniques and precise control over optical properties of materials at the nanoscale.

7Build Process

A multi-step process including wafer preparation, lithography, deposition, patterning, etching, and assembly of photonic elements onto electronic circuits.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Light source — supplies coherent light to carry and process signals. Key parts: external laser or laser array, edge or grating couplers, on-chip amplifiers.
  • Input encoding — converts electronic data into optical signals. Key parts: Mach-Zehnder modulators or microring modulators, driver electronics, bias control.
  • Photonic compute mesh — performs matrix multiplication as light interferes through a network of tunable elements. Key parts: interferometer mesh (Mach-Zehnder or microring arrays), phase shifters, waveguides.
  • Weight storage and programming — sets the matrix values that the light sees. Key parts: thermo-optic or electro-optic phase tuners, non-volatile phase-change cells (lab stage), calibration DACs.
  • Detection and output — turns the resulting optical intensities back into electronic signals. Key parts: germanium photodiodes, transimpedance amplifiers, analog-to-digital converters.
  • Electronic control and calibration — keeps the mesh stable against temperature drift and fabrication variation. Key parts: control loops, monitor photodiodes, digital interface.
  • Packaging and thermal management — brings fibre, electronics and cooling together in a usable module. Key parts: fibre arrays, flip-chip or co-packaged electronics, heat spreaders.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Design the mesh and map the network. A neural-network layer is factored into a sequence of matrix operations and mapped onto an interferometer or microring layout, with the required precision chosen.
  2. Fabricate the photonic chip. Silicon-on-insulator wafers are patterned in a photonic foundry using deep-ultraviolet lithography and etching to form waveguides, splitters and interferometers.
  3. Add tunable phase shifters. Heaters or doped silicon regions are deposited and patterned to change the refractive index; non-volatile phase-change materials are an alternative in research.
  4. Integrate light sources. Lasers are attached by hybrid bonding, edge coupling or grating coupling, since silicon itself does not emit light efficiently.
  5. Integrate photodetectors. Germanium is grown or bonded at the outputs, then connected to amplifiers and analog-to-digital converters.
  6. Co-package with electronics. Driver, control and readout chips are flip-chipped or co-packaged close to the photonic die to keep electrical paths short.
  7. Calibrate every element. Each interferometer is tuned while measuring its output, compensating for nanometre-scale fabrication errors and thermal crosstalk — the hardest step in practice.
  8. Test inference accuracy. Real neural-network tasks are run and compared with a digital reference, checking that analog noise and drift do not break the result.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Silicon photonics manufacturing — the same foundries that make optical transceivers can pattern waveguides and modulators at scale.
  • EXISTS High-speed modulators and photodetectors — they convert data into light and back at tens of gigabits per second.
  • PARTIAL On-chip or hybrid lasers — silicon cannot lase efficiently, so lasers must be bonded or coupled on, which adds cost and loss.
  • NOT YET Non-volatile optical weights — today's phase shifters need constant power and drift; phase-change materials could hold weights without power but are not yet reliable.
  • PARTIAL Electronic-photonic co-integration — control and readout electronics must sit beside the photonics without disturbing the light.
  • NOT YET Optical nonlinearities and memory — a fully optical neural network needs activation functions and storage in the optical domain, which remain laboratory curiosities.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: laboratory and startup prototypes multiply small matrices optically — proof that interference-based math can match digital results for narrow tasks.
  2. Next: hybrid accelerators that offload only linear layers while electronics handle nonlinearities — lower energy per inference for large language models in datacentres.
  3. Next: co-packaged photonic tiles with integrated lasers, detectors and control — deployment inside standard servers without exotic optics.
  4. Longer term: non-volatile optical weights and on-chip optical nonlinearities — deeper all-optical networks with much lower power per operation.
