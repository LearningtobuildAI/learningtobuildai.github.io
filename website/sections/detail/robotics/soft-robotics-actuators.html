PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Soft Robotics Actuators are flexible, compliant robotic components made from elastomers that can mimic the movement patterns of biological muscles, enabling them to interact safely with delicate objects or environments.

Category
Robotics
Best use
Fragile object handling, Medical surgery
Stage

2Problem It Solves

They address the need for robots that can safely interact with humans and delicate materials, such as in medical procedures or food processing, where traditional rigid robotic systems could cause harm or damage due to their inflexibility and high force capabilities.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

These actuators use pneumatic or hydraulic inflation of chambered polymers to achieve non-linear motion. This allows for a range of movements that are more akin to human muscle actions and can provide gentle contact forces suitable for handling fragile objects without causing damage.

5Materials Used
  • elastomers
  • polymer compounds
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

Manufacturing involves molding elastomers into complex shapes using molds. The process requires precise control over material composition and curing conditions to ensure the actuators have the desired mechanical properties.

7Build Process

The build process typically includes designing the actuator geometry, selecting appropriate polymers based on performance requirements, creating molds, injecting or casting the polymer into the mold, and then post-processing steps like curing and testing for functionality and durability.

8Energy Requirements

Field units draw low hundreds of watts; fabrication is energy-intensive due to vacuum baking and precise molding processes.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Actuator body — the soft muscle itself — elastomer chambers that deform when pressurised or electrically excited. Key parts: silicone or thermoplastic elastomer, fibre reinforcement that steers the motion, internal channels or electrode layers.
  • Pressure or field supply — inflates the chambers, or charges electrodes in electrically driven designs. Key parts: compressor or pump, miniature solenoid valves, pressure regulator, high-voltage supply for dielectric elastomer or electrohydraulic actuators.
  • Sensing — soft bodies bend in ways rigid sensors cannot measure directly. Key parts: stretchable strain sensors, pressure and flow sensors, capacitive tactile skin, cameras for external pose estimation.
  • Control — commands are non-linear, so the controller must model or learn the body's behaviour. Key parts: microcontroller, hyperelastic finite-element model or learned model, closed-loop pressure or voltage regulation.
  • Structure and interface — connects the soft actuator to a gripper, limb or tool and takes the load. Key parts: mounting plate, compliant joints, tubing or wiring harness.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Design the chamber geometry. Channels, wall thickness and fibre winding are chosen so internal pressure produces the wanted bend, twist or extension.
  2. Make a mould. A two-part mould is machined or 3D-printed; complex internal channels are cast around a dissolvable or wax core.
  3. Cast the elastomer. Mixed silicone is degassed under vacuum and poured, then cured in an oven.
  4. Bond the layers. Multiple cast halves are joined with uncured silicone as adhesive, and reinforcing fibres or fabric are wrapped around or embedded.
  5. Add sensing and electrodes. Stretchable conductors, strain gauges or electrode layers are printed, sprayed or cast into the walls.
  6. Plumb and wire up. Tubing, valves and electrical connections are fitted, and the actuator is leak-tested.
  7. Characterise. Blocked force, free displacement and fatigue life are measured on a test rig over many cycles.
  8. Tune the controller. A model or learned policy is fitted to the measurements, then closed-loop control is validated on the target task.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Fatigue-resistant elastomers — soft actuators must survive millions of inflate-deflate cycles without tearing.
  • PARTIAL Miniature valves and pumps — today's compressors and valves are bulky, so most soft robots are tethered to off-board air.
  • PARTIAL Stretchable electronics and sensors — sensing that bends with the body is needed for closed-loop control.
  • PARTIAL Soft-body modelling and simulation — hyperelastic materials deform non-linearly, making real-time control hard.
  • PARTIAL High-voltage, compact drivers — electrically driven elastomer actuators need kilovolt-class supplies small enough to carry.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: commercially sold soft grippers for food, agriculture and fragile packaging — handling of items that rigid jaws damage or cannot grasp.
  2. Next: untethered soft actuators with onboard power and sensing — mobile robots, wearable assistive gloves and exosuits.
  3. Next: soft instruments in surgical and endoscopic robots — safer interaction with tissue and access through narrow, curved paths.
  4. Longer term: durable, self-sensing artificial muscle with plant-like fatigue life — prosthetics and humanoid limbs that move and feel more like biological muscle.

18Glossary
Elastomer
A type of polymer that can be stretched or deformed significantly and will return to its original shape when the stress is removed.
Pneumatic
Relating to air or other gases under pressure, often used in systems where fluid power is employed for actuation.
Hydraulic
Relating to the mechanical power of liquids, especially fluids confined in a container or pipe system.
