PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

These micro-bots communicate using stigmergic patterns, similar to how ant colonies optimize their behavior. They locate damage through sensors, then collectively repair it by depositing materials like resin or sealing agents.

5Materials Used
  • resin
  • sealing agents
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The micro-bots are manufactured in small, compact units with minimal components. They require precise assembly techniques but can be produced at scale using standard manufacturing processes.

7Build Process

Micro-bots are built through a combination of microfabrication and additive manufacturing. Each bot is equipped with sensors, communication modules, and actuators for movement and material deposition.

8Energy Requirements

Field units draw low hundreds of watts; fabrication is energy-intensive due to vacuum baking. Operation requires minimal power as bots are designed to be energy-efficient.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Micro-robot units — the individual bots that carry out inspection and repair. Key parts: locomotion mechanism (vibration, legs, or magnetic), onboard sensing (crack detection), repair tool (material extruder or heater), microcontroller and battery.
  • Swarm coordination — lets thousands of simple bots work together without a central controller. Key parts: stigmergic markers (physical or digital traces), local communication (acoustic or radio), ant colony optimization algorithms.
  • Power and energy — keeps the bots running long enough to complete repairs. Key parts: high-density microbatteries, energy harvesting (vibration or thermal), wireless power transfer (optional).
  • Repair payload — the material that seals cracks and restores structural integrity. Key parts: fast-curing polymer or cementitious grout, micro-valve or nozzle, curing trigger (heat or UV).
  • Navigation and localization — enables the swarm to map and navigate inside dark, GPS-denied pipes or roads. Key parts: inertial measurement units, visual or lidar sensors, magnetic field sensing, map-building algorithms.
  • Deployment and retrieval — gets the swarm into and out of the infrastructure. Key parts: access ports, launch canisters, retrieval nets or vacuums.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Design the micro-robot. Define size (millimetres to centimetres), locomotion for pipes or road surfaces, and payload capacity. Use MEMS fabrication or precision assembly.
  2. Develop the repair payload. Formulate a material that can be dispensed in small amounts, cures quickly at ambient or triggered temperatures, and bonds to concrete or asphalt.
  3. Implement stigmergic communication. Choose a marker system: physical trails (e.g., magnetic or chemical) or digital pheromones shared via local radio. Code ant colony optimization for task allocation.
  4. Build swarm control algorithms. Simulate thousands of agents to tune rules for exploration, crack detection, and repair without collisions.
  5. Fabricate prototypes. Produce a few hundred robots using micro-manufacturing, integrating power, sensing, and repair tools.
  6. Test in laboratory pipes. Release swarms into transparent test pipes with artificial cracks, measuring repair success and swarm efficiency.
  7. Conduct field trials. Deploy in decommissioned or active pipes and road sections under supervision, iterating on reliability.
  8. Integrate with city maintenance. Develop deployment protocols, user interfaces for operators, and standards for repaired infrastructure.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Micro-robotics — small robots exist for inspection, but none combine locomotion, repair, and power at the needed scale.
  • PARTIAL Swarm intelligence algorithms — ant colony optimization is well studied, but applying it to physical repair with thousands of units is untested.
  • PARTIAL Rapid-curing repair materials — self-healing concretes and grouts exist, but dispensing them from micro-bots is unproven.
  • NOT YET Micro-scale power sources — batteries small enough for millimetre bots cannot yet store enough energy for long missions.
  • NOT YET Communication in confined spaces — radio signals fade in metal pipes; acoustic or magnetic methods are experimental.
  • NOT YET Deployment and retrieval systems — no standard way to insert and recover thousands of bots from city infrastructure.
12Theoretical Path to Reality
  1. Now: laboratory demonstrations of small robot swarms repairing simple cracks in pipes — proof of concept for coordinated repair.
  2. Next: field pilots in controlled pipe networks and road sections — data on reliability, speed, and material performance.
  3. Next: swarms of hundreds operating in live infrastructure under human supervision — commercial services for targeted repairs.
  4. Longer term: autonomous thousands-strong swarms integrated into city maintenance — continuous, low-disruption repair of buried infrastructure.
