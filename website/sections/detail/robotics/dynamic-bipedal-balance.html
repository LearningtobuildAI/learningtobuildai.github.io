4How It Works

The system employs Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithms to continuously adjust the center of mass and joint angles of the robot at millisecond intervals. This allows for smooth navigation and stability even when traversing irregular surfaces.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Partly there

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • State estimation — works out where the body is, how it is tilted and where the feet are touching. Key parts: inertial measurement unit (1 kHz), joint encoders, foot force/torque sensors, depth camera or LiDAR, sensor-fusion filter.
  • Terrain and contact model — predicts where the next foot can land and how much it will grip. Key parts: local height map, footstep planner, slip and friction estimate, obstacle grid.
  • Centroidal planner (MPC) — re-plans the centre of mass and foot forces over a short horizon, hundreds of times a second. Key parts: single-rigid-body dynamics model, quadratic program solver, friction-cone and zero-moment-point constraints, contact schedule.
  • Whole-body controller — converts the plan into torque commands at every joint. Key parts: inverse dynamics or task-priority QP, swing-leg trajectory generator, joint torque limits, actuator command bus.
  • Actuators and limbs — deliver large, fast, back-drivable torque at the ankle, knee and hip. Key parts: quasi-direct-drive motors, low-ratio planetary or harmonic gears, torque sensors, lightweight carbon-fibre or aluminium links.
  • Reflex and safety layer — catches the pushes and trips that the slow planner cannot. Key parts: capture-point step recovery, swing-leg retraction, fall-safe crouch, emergency stop.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Model the body. Measure every link's mass and inertia and write the equations of motion, including contact with the ground, into a physics simulator such as MuJoCo.
  2. Instrument the machine. Fit an inertial measurement unit on the pelvis, encoders at each joint and force sensors in the feet, then calibrate them against each other.
  3. Build the state estimator. Fuse inertial data with leg kinematics so the robot knows its pelvis velocity and foot positions; handle the sharp impact spikes when a foot lands.
  4. Write the balance planner. Approximate the robot as a point mass on an extendable leg, predict its motion 0.5–1 s ahead, and solve a constrained optimisation each control tick for the centre-of-mass acceleration and foot forces.
  5. Add the whole-body controller. Turn the plan into joint torques by inverse dynamics or a task-priority quadratic program, respecting torque, joint-limit and self-collision constraints.
  6. Grow the reflex layer. Add fast heuristics — capture-point stepping, swing-leg retraction — that act within one or two ticks when the robot is pushed.
  7. Train and validate in simulation. Run millions of simulated steps, randomising mass, friction, latency and sensor noise, so the controller survives conditions it was not tuned for.
  8. Test in escalating stages. Standing, then push recovery on a harness, then flat-floor walking, then ramps, rubble and stairs, always with an overhead gantry or soft floor as a fallback.
  9. Deploy with limits. Derate torque and speed in the field, log every contact event, and keep a remote operator able to halt the robot.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS High-torque-density, force-controllable actuators — balance needs joints that can be commanded in torque and back-driven, not just positioned.
  • EXISTS Real-time compute — the optimisation and controller must finish in well under one control tick, a few hundred to a few thousand times per second.
  • EXISTS Cheap inertial and force sensing — without reliable tilt and foot-load data the estimator drifts and the robot falls.
  • PARTIAL Fast physics simulation and sim-to-real transfer — most control policies are learned or tuned in simulation and still suffer from the reality gap.
  • PARTIAL Energy storage light enough to carry — untethered operation for a full shift is still limited by battery mass.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: humanoids in warehouses doing structured tote and kitting tasks on flat floors — early commercial value where the ground is predictable.
  2. Next: reliable balance on gravel, slopes, stairs and after being pushed by a person — construction sites, plant rooms and last-metre delivery.
  3. Next: learned policies combined with model-based MPC — recovery from slips and unseen terrain without hand-written rules.
  4. Longer term: fleet-scale reliability and lower unit cost — many robots per site, maintained like other industrial machinery.

18Glossary
Model Predictive Control (MPC)
A control strategy that uses a mathematical model of the system to predict future behavior and optimize control inputs in real-time.
Center of Mass
The point at which all mass of an object can be considered to be concentrated for the purpose of understanding its motion or balance.
