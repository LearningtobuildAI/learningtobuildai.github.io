9Engineering Breakdown
  • Bidirectional charger — converts grid AC to DC to charge the battery, and DC back to AC to export power. Key parts: AC/DC converter, DC/DC converter, isolation transformer, cooling, controller.
  • Vehicle battery and management system — stores energy and must accept controlled discharge without excessive degradation. Key parts: lithium-ion pack, battery management system (BMS), thermal management.
  • Communication and control link — lets the vehicle, charger, aggregator and grid operator agree on power flows. Key parts: ISO 15118-20 (V2G) or CHAdeMO protocol, OCPP for charger-to-backend, smart meter, secure gateway.
  • Aggregation platform — groups many parked EVs into a single controllable resource large enough to matter to the grid. Key parts: cloud dispatch software, forecasting and optimisation, cybersecurity.
  • Grid interface and protection — connects the charger safely to the local distribution network. Key parts: switchgear, protection relays, islanding detection, metering.
  • Market and utility integration — turns aggregated capacity into paid grid services. Key parts: demand-response signals, frequency and voltage support contracts, settlement systems.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Agree on standards. Adopt a common V2G communication standard, such as ISO 15118-20, so any compliant car can talk to any compliant charger.
  2. Design the bidirectional charger. Build a power converter that can both rectify and invert, with isolation and safety interlocks for back-feeding the grid.
  3. Integrate with the vehicle. Work with automakers so the battery management system permits controlled discharge and warranties cover the extra cycling.
  4. Install chargers and connections. Fit bidirectional wallboxes or fleet depot chargers, each with its own protection and metering.
  5. Deploy the aggregation layer. Run software that pools hundreds or thousands of vehicles, forecasts their availability and bids their capacity into grid markets.
  6. Register with the grid operator. Meet local connection rules, demonstrate anti-islanding and protection, and sign up to frequency-response or demand-response programmes.
  7. Pilot and scale. Start with fleets and committed drivers, measure battery wear, then widen to homes and workplaces.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Bidirectional charging hardware — available in some models and chargers, but not yet standard equipment.
  • PARTIAL V2G communication standards — ISO 15118-20 and CHAdeMO exist, but support across cars and chargers is uneven.
  • PARTIAL Battery and warranty acceptance — automakers must allow deep discharge and cover the resulting degradation.
  • PARTIAL Aggregation and market rules — grid operators need rules and payment mechanisms for millions of small storage devices.
  • EXISTS Smart metering and grid visibility — distribution networks must see and manage power flows at the edge.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: fleet and home V2G pilots with a few thousand vehicles — real data on battery wear, driver behaviour and grid value.
  2. Next: standardised V2G in mass-market cars and chargers — any compatible vehicle can earn money while parked.
  3. Next: commercial aggregation at scale — EV fleets act as flexible storage that helps balance renewables.
  4. Longer term: V2G as a default grid resource — cheaper grid operation, less peaking plant and stronger renewable integration.
