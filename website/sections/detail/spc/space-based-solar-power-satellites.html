PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Space-Based Solar Power Satellites refer to large-scale satellite systems designed to capture solar energy from the sun, which is continuously available in space due to its proximity to the sun compared to Earth's intermittent daylight. This captured energy would then be transmitted back to Earth for use.

2Problem It Solves

The primary issue addressed is the potential for a more reliable and consistent source of solar energy compared to terrestrial systems, which suffer from weather conditions and night-time limitations. Additionally, it aims to provide a significant increase in available power by utilizing the sun's full output without atmospheric interference.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

These satellites utilize advanced photovoltaic panels to convert sunlight into electricity. The generated power can be transmitted back to Earth via wireless means, such as microwaves or lasers, which are directed towards receiving stations on the ground.

5Materials Used
  • high-strength composites
  • solar cells
  • heat-resistant coatings
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

Manufacturing involves creating large, robust satellites capable of withstanding the harsh space environment, integrating advanced photovoltaic technology, and developing efficient wireless power transmission systems. Materials such as high-strength composites, solar cells, and heat-resistant coatings are key components.

7Build Process

The build process includes designing the satellite structure, assembling the photovoltaic panels, testing the electrical systems, and conducting space simulation tests to ensure reliability in orbit. Launching the satellites into geostationary or low Earth orbits is a critical step followed by deployment and integration with ground receiving stations.

14Glossary
photovoltaic panels
Devices that convert light energy into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect.
wireless power transfer
Technologies for transmitting electrical energy without physical contact, often using electromagnetic fields or lasers.
geostationary orbit
A satellite orbit where the orbital period is equal to Earth's rotational period, resulting in a fixed position relative to the ground.
microwaves
Electromagnetic waves used for wireless power transmission over long distances.
