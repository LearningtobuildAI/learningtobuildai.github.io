PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Artificial Consciousness refers to the development of AI systems capable of experiencing, understanding, and learning about their environment in a manner that mimics human consciousness. This involves creating machines that can not only process information but also have subjective experiences and feelings.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Not yet possible

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Theory of consciousness — a formal, testable account of what the property is, so there is something to build toward rather than just a word. Key parts: global workspace and broadcast, integrated information, attention schema, predictive processing.
  • Cognitive architecture — the software that would exhibit the property. Key parts: deep or spiking neural networks, recurrent loops, a shared workspace layer between specialised modules, self-model.
  • Embodiment and closed loop — sensors and actuators so the system acts in a world and its own actions change what it senses. Key parts: cameras, microphones, touch, motors or simulated body, real-time control loop.
  • Biological substrate — the hybrid route: living neurons wired to electronics. Key parts: human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids, multi-electrode arrays, microfluidic life support, stimulation and recording hardware.
  • Measurement and perturbation — instruments for testing whether a system has the property, not just whether it behaves cleverly. Key parts: perturbational complexity index, causal analysis of neural signals, behavioural report tasks.
  • Governance and containment — oversight, monitoring and a kill switch before anyone makes a claim. Key parts: ethics review, activity monitoring, shutdown and containment.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Turn a theory into an algorithm. Express a theory — global workspace, integrated information, attention schema — as precise code that predicts measurable differences between a system that has the property and one that does not. Nothing can be built until this exists.
  2. Design the architecture. Assemble a large recurrent network with a bottleneck or 'workspace' layer that many specialised modules write into and read from, rather than a stack of feed-forward layers.
  3. Close the loop with a body. Connect the model to sensors and actuators, real or simulated, so its output changes its input. Most theories tie the property to a system that models itself as an agent in a world.
  4. Grow the biological alternative. Culture human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids on multi-electrode arrays, keep them alive with microfluidics, and wire stimulation to their own recorded activity so they learn through feedback. This is the hybrid route.
  5. Measure indicators, not behaviour. Apply the perturbational complexity index: deliver a magnetic or electrical pulse, record how widespread and how complex the echo is, and compare against awake and anaesthetised human brains.
  6. Run adversarial tests. Check that simple feed-forward systems and look-up tables score low, and that anything scoring high does so for the right reason. A measure that can be gamed is worthless.
  7. Scale toward whole-brain emulation. Map a real brain's wiring, then simulate it at cellular detail in real time. Only a handful of very small nervous systems are anywhere near this today, and the compute gap is orders of magnitude.
  8. Set oversight before publication. Independent ethics review, containment, and a policy on what may be claimed. This step exists legally and scientifically, not just morally.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Testable theories of consciousness — several compete (global workspace, integrated information, attention schema) and none commands consensus, so the target is not yet well defined.
  • EXISTS Indicators that distinguish conscious from unconscious systems — the perturbational complexity index already separates awake from anaesthetised human brains in clinical research, giving a starting yardstick.
  • EXISTS Large-scale neural compute — training and running very large networks on accelerators is routine, though nowhere near real-time whole-brain simulation.
  • PARTIAL Human brain organoids on electrode arrays — small cortical organoids have been cultured and trained in closed loops, but they are millimetre-scale, short-lived and lack defined circuits.
  • NOT YET Whole-brain connectomes and emulation — complete wiring maps exist only for small organisms; no mammal brain has been mapped or simulated at that detail.
12Theoretical Path to Reality
  1. Now: competing theories formalised and compared against brain recordings, with complexity indices used clinically — arguing about measurable indicators instead of definitions.
  2. Next: software models and organoid-silicon hybrids that reproduce those indicators on simple tasks — candidate test beds — though reproducing an indicator is not the same as having the property.
  3. Next: whole-brain emulation of a small animal in real time — a benchmark for whether simulating a brain produces a mind, and a hard test of the theories.
  4. Longer term: contested claims, governance frameworks and no clear product — a decision society has to make about research limits; there is no grid, drug or device at the end of this path.
