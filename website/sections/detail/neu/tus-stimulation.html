PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Partly there

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Transducer array — generates the ultrasound beam and steers it electronically to a deep target. Key parts: piezoelectric elements (lead zirconate titanate or single-crystal), backing and matching layers, array housing.
  • Drive electronics — generate the shaped electrical pulses delivered to each element. Key parts: multi-channel amplifiers, phase and amplitude controllers, pulse programmer.
  • Targeting and acoustic modelling — works out where the beam actually lands after the skull distorts it. Key parts: CT and MRI scans, skull-thickness and density model, wave-propagation simulation, neuronavigation registration.
  • Coupling and positioning — gets sound into the head without air gaps and holds the array in place. Key parts: hydrogel or gel pad, positioning arm or robot, strap or frame.
  • Monitoring and safety interlocks — detects unwanted heating or neural effects and stops delivery. Key parts: thermometry or acoustic feedback, EEG or EMG, dose-logging software.
  • Protocol and control software — defines pulse patterns and enforces limits on intensity and duty cycle. Key parts: pulse-sequence library, dose calculator, sham and blinding controls.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Fabricate the array. Piezoelectric material is diced into many elements, wired individually, and potted into a housing that fits against the scalp.
  2. Characterise the beam. The array is scanned in a water tank to map its pressure field, focal size and sidelobes at each steering setting.
  3. Model the individual skull. A CT scan gives skull thickness and density; software simulates how much the bone slows, absorbs and bends the beam.
  4. Correct the phases. Each element's phase and amplitude are adjusted so the distorted waves still arrive in step at the chosen target.
  5. Register and position. The array is aligned to the patient's MRI using neuronavigation, then coupled to the scalp with gel or a hydrogel pad.
  6. Select a dose. Frequency, intensity, pulse length, duty cycle and train duration are chosen low enough to be non-thermal but strong enough to shift neuronal activity.
  7. Deliver and monitor. Pulses are delivered in short trains while EEG and device readouts watch for unintended effects or heating.
  8. Verify the effect. Change in brain activity is checked by functional imaging or electrophysiology, and the result is compared against a sham condition.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Phased-array piezoelectric transducers — they allow electronic steering of a focus through the skull without moving the device.
  • PARTIAL Skull aberration correction — bone distorts and absorbs the beam, so targeting depends on CT-based modelling that is still imperfect.
  • EXISTS Structural and functional imaging — MRI and CT are needed to pick and verify a deep target.
  • PARTIAL Mechanobiology of mechanosensitive channels — the mechanism is thought to involve membrane displacement and channels such as Piezo1, but details at the doses used remain unsettled.
  • PARTIAL Dosimetry and safety standards for neuromodulation — standards exist for diagnostic imaging and ablation, not for repeated low-intensity brain stimulation.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: small human trials targeting deep structures such as the thalamus, hippocampus and prefrontal cortex — evidence that the technique can change activity in chosen circuits safely.
  2. Next: larger sham-controlled trials for depression, epilepsy and chronic pain — separation of real effect from placebo and confirmation of parameter sets.
  3. Next: closed-loop systems that read brain activity and adjust the beam automatically — more precise, personalised dosing.
  4. Longer term: regulatory clearance for a neuromodulation indication — clinical adoption; high-intensity ultrasound ablation of deep brain targets is already clinically used for tremor, which de-risks the delivery hardware.
