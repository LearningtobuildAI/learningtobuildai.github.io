9Engineering Breakdown
  • Learner model — maintains an evolving estimate of what the student knows, their misconceptions, and their cognitive and affective state. Key parts: knowledge tracing model (e.g. Bayesian or deep knowledge tracing), affect classifier using text, voice or facial cues, skill and concept mapping.
  • Domain knowledge graph — organises curriculum concepts and their prerequisite relationships so the system knows what can be taught next. Key parts: concept nodes, prerequisite edges, linked learning resources and questions.
  • Adaptive engine — selects the next activity, difficulty, and pedagogical strategy in real time. Key parts: policy model (reinforcement learning or rule-based), mastery thresholds, constraints from curriculum standards.
  • Conversational interface — delivers instruction, hints and feedback in natural language. Key parts: large language model, retrieval-augmented generation over trusted content, speech recognition and synthesis for spoken dialogue.
  • Assessment and diagnostics — measures learning and detects confusion, frustration or disengagement during a session. Key parts: auto-graded questions, open-response analysis, real-time sentiment and affect analysis.
  • Data and privacy layer — logs interactions, retrains models, and protects sensitive student data. Key parts: event streaming pipeline, anonymisation and consent management, access controls and audit logs.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Define learning objectives and build a concept graph. Subject experts break a curriculum into fine-grained skills and map prerequisite links between them.
  2. Collect and curate training data. Student responses, timings and expert annotations are gathered and labelled to train models; data must be representative and privacy-cleared.
  3. Train knowledge tracing and affect models. Statistical and neural models learn to infer mastery and emotional state from sequences of interactions.
  4. Develop the adaptive policy. A decision engine is built — often by simulating student behaviour or using reinforcement learning — to choose what to present and when to switch strategy.
  5. Integrate an LLM with retrieval-augmented generation. The language model is grounded in vetted curriculum content so its explanations stay accurate and age-appropriate.
  6. Build the real-time feedback loop. Sentiment analysis, response latency and error patterns feed back into the adaptive engine within seconds to adjust pacing or tone.
  7. Create teacher and student dashboards. Interfaces show progress, flag struggling students, and let teachers override or inspect the AI's decisions.
  8. Run pilots and controlled trials. Deploy in classrooms or corporate training, compare learning gains against conventional instruction, and measure engagement and equity.
  9. Deploy with privacy safeguards and monitoring. Roll out with consent management, data minimisation, and continuous checks for bias, drift and harmful outputs.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Large language models — they provide fluent explanations and dialogue, but need grounding to avoid factual errors.
  • EXISTS Knowledge tracing algorithms — they estimate a student's mastery over time from their answers, forming the core of personalisation.
  • PARTIAL Affect detection from text, voice or video — accuracy is still limited and raises privacy concerns, so real-time emotional adaptation is not yet reliable.
  • EXISTS Retrieval-augmented generation — it lets the tutor cite trusted curriculum content instead of relying on the model's memory alone.
  • PARTIAL Privacy-preserving machine learning — student data is sensitive and often regulated; techniques like federated learning are still maturing for this use.
  • PARTIAL Large-scale, diverse educational datasets — models trained on narrow populations may not generalise, and labelled data for cognitive patterns is scarce.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: AI tutors that adapt difficulty and pacing based on correctness and response time — widely deployed homework help and corporate microlearning.
  2. Next: multimodal affect detection integrated into the adaptation loop — tutors that notice frustration or boredom and change tone or activity in real time.
  3. Next: deep integration with school systems and teacher oversight tools — blended classrooms where the AI handles practice while teachers focus on mentoring.
  4. Longer term: portable lifelong learner profiles spanning platforms — continuous personalised education from school into career, with the student in control of their data.
