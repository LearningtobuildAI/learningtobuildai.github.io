PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

A direct fusion drive uses a compact fusion reactor that exhausts its own high-velocity plasma as propellant, delivering electrical power and thrust from one machine. It would cut outer solar system transit times from years to months.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

A field-reversed configuration or similar plasma is heated to fusion conditions and confined magnetically, and the escaping plasma is shaped and expanded by a magnetic nozzle. The result is moderate thrust at very high exhaust velocity, which suits long-duration transfers more than surface launch.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Not yet possible

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Fusion core — a compact field-reversed configuration: a plasma torus with closed field lines and no toroidal coils through its centre. Key parts: field-reversed plasma torus, solenoidal coil set, rotating-magnetic-field antenna, gas injection.
  • Superconducting magnets — generate the steady field that confines and stabilises the plasma. Key parts: high-temperature superconducting coils, cryostat and cryocooler.
  • Heating and fuelling — raises the plasma to fusion temperature and feeds it deuterium and helium-3. Key parts: radio-frequency heating, pellet or gas injectors, deuterium and helium-3 storage.
  • Magnetic nozzle — shapes the escaping plasma into a directed exhaust, converting thermal energy into thrust. Key parts: expanding field coils, plasma-facing throat, thrust-measuring instruments.
  • Power conversion — draws electricity from the same reaction for the spacecraft's systems. Key parts: turbine and alternator, or direct conversion of charged fusion products, heat exchangers, radiators.
  • Shielding and fuel cycle — protects the vehicle from the neutrons that side reactions still produce, and manages scarce helium-3. Key parts: neutron shield, tritium handling, helium-3 tanks.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Settle the plasma physics. Small experiments such as the Princeton Field-Reversed Configuration must show that an FRC can be formed, sustained and heated by rotating magnetic fields without falling apart.
  2. Design the burn. Choose fuel and operating point. Deuterium–helium-3 is favoured because most of its energy leaves as charged particles that a magnetic nozzle can steer, but it needs far higher temperatures than deuterium–tritium and still produces some neutrons.
  3. Wind the magnets. High-temperature superconducting coils are wound and potted so they keep their shape under the forces of a high field, then enclosed in a cryostat.
  4. Build the core vessel. A vacuum chamber is made with a constricted throat at the exhaust end and fitted with radio-frequency antennas and injectors.
  5. Add the nozzle and instrumentation. Expanding coils are mounted downstream of the throat, with probes and a thrust stand to measure what the exhaust actually does in vacuum.
  6. Close the power loop. Heat is taken from the machine into a turbine and alternator, and waste heat is rejected through radiators. The electricity partly feeds back into the magnets, heating and controls.
  7. Test on the ground, then in orbit. A prototype is run in a large vacuum chamber, measuring thrust, specific impulse and net power over long durations. Only then does a flight demonstration make sense.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Field-reversed configuration confinement and stability — FRCs have been created and sustained in laboratory devices, but not held in a hot, fusion-burning state.
  • NOT YET Aneutronic or low-neutron fusion conditions — deuterium–helium-3 fusion needs temperatures well above deuterium–tritium, which itself has not yet produced net power in a magnetic device.
  • PARTIAL High-temperature superconducting magnets — compact, high-field coils are what make a small fusion core conceivable at all.
  • PARTIAL Magnetic nozzles for plasma exhaust — demonstrated in laboratory plasma thrusters, but never with a fusion-grade exhaust.
  • NOT YET Helium-3 supply — helium-3 is extremely scarce on Earth, so the concept assumes extraction from lunar regolith or planetary atmospheres that does not exist.
  • PARTIAL Lightweight space radiators and shielding — a fusion drive must reject waste heat and absorb neutrons without carrying tonnes of inert mass.
12Theoretical Path to Reality
  1. Now: laboratory FRC experiments and paper studies of direct fusion drive missions — a physics basis and plausible mission profiles.
  2. Next: a ground prototype that sustains a hot FRC and produces measurable thrust in a vacuum chamber — confidence that one machine can give both power and propulsion.
  3. Next: an in-space demonstration of a small drive — real data on reliability, heat rejection and thrust over months.
  4. Longer term: operational drives for outer solar system transport — transit times of months rather than years, and abundant power at the destination — but not Earth launch, since thrust-to-weight is far too low.
