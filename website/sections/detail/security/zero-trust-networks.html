PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Available today

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Identity provider — proves who a user or machine is before anything else happens. Key parts: directory of identities, multi-factor authentication, OpenID Connect and SAML federation, short-lived tokens.
  • Policy engine and administrator — decides, per request, whether this identity on this device may reach this resource now. Key parts: policy language and rules, risk signals, central administration.
  • Policy enforcement points — sit in front of each resource and allow or block traffic on the policy engine's verdict. Key parts: identity-aware proxy, API gateway, load balancer.
  • Device posture and inventory — only known, patched, managed devices are treated as trustworthy. Key parts: endpoint management agent, device certificates, attestation.
  • Microsegmentation fabric — splits the internal network so a compromise in one service cannot move freely. Key parts: per-workload firewalls, service identity and mutual TLS, software-defined network policy.
  • Telemetry and analytics — gives the policy engine live evidence and catches behaviour that policy missed. Key parts: log collection, behavioural baselines, alerting and response.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Inventory assets and data flows. Map every user, service, device and the data they touch. Policy cannot be written for systems nobody has catalogued.
  2. Give every actor a strong identity. Enrol staff in single sign-on with multi-factor authentication, and issue cryptographic identities to servers, containers and workloads as well as people.
  3. Register and assess devices. Enrol laptops and phones in management, install device certificates, and define the minimum patch level and configuration a device needs.
  4. Stand up the policy decision point. Deploy a central engine that evaluates identity, device posture and context, and writes rules such as 'finance staff on managed devices, during work hours, for these applications'.
  5. Insert enforcement points. Put identity-aware proxies or gateways in front of each application so every request is checked, rather than trusting anything already inside the network.
  6. Microsegment the internal network. Replace flat internal routing with per-workload rules and mutual TLS, so east-west traffic is authenticated too.
  7. Retire perimeter-only access. Move users off blanket VPN access to per-application, least-privilege, just-in-time connections, keeping break-glass accounts for outages.
  8. Instrument and tune continuously. Feed logs and alerts back into policy, tighten rules that prove unnecessary, and rehearse what happens when the identity system itself fails.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Public-key cryptography and hardware-backed credentials — phishing-resistant factors and device certificates are the root of every trust decision.
  • EXISTS Open standards for identity and federation — OpenID Connect, SAML and FIDO2/WebAuthn let many vendors' components interoperate.
  • EXISTS Identity-aware proxies and gateways — they are the enforcement points that make per-request checking practical.
  • PARTIAL Certificate and key lifecycle automation — short-lived credentials only work if issuance and renewal are automatic.
  • NOT YET Legacy and industrial systems that support modern authentication — many industrial control, medical and mainframe systems cannot present a modern identity, forcing exceptions.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: identity-first deployments: single sign-on, universal MFA, per-application access replacing blanket VPN — a big reduction in credential theft and lateral movement.
  2. Next: phishing-resistant authentication such as passkeys and hardware security keys as the default, plus device attestation — defence against credential phishing and stolen-session attacks.
  3. Next: workload identity and mutual TLS across data centres and clouds — microsegmentation that does not depend on network location.
  4. Longer term: continuous, risk-adaptive policy with automated containment, extended to operational technology — access decisions that tighten in real time when behaviour looks wrong, across factories and critical infrastructure as well as offices.
