PART 3Engineering & Build Guide

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Sensing layer — detects physiological signals such as heart rate, muscle activity, temperature and motion. Key parts: graphene strain sensors, graphene electrodes for ECG/EMG, temperature-sensitive graphene patterns.
  • Conductive network — carries electrical signals from sensors to the processing unit without bulky wires. Key parts: graphene-coated yarns, printed graphene traces, stretchable interconnects.
  • Textile substrate — hosts the electronics while remaining comfortable, breathable and stretchable. Key parts: woven or knitted fabric, elastic base layer.
  • Electronics module — processes sensor data and transmits it wirelessly. Key parts: microcontroller, Bluetooth Low Energy chip, flexible battery.
  • Encapsulation — protects the electronics and graphene from sweat and repeated washing. Key parts: waterproof polymer coating, flexible encapsulant.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Produce graphene. Graphene is made by chemical vapour deposition on metal foil, by reducing graphene oxide, or by liquid-phase exfoliation to create a printable ink.
  2. Formulate ink or coating. Graphene is dispersed in a solvent with binders and surfactants to make a stable ink or sprayable coating.
  3. Apply to textile. The ink is screen-printed, dip-coated or sprayed onto fabric, or mixed into polymer and spun into fibres.
  4. Pattern sensors and interconnects. Masks or direct printing create specific sensor shapes and conductive paths that connect them.
  5. Integrate electronics. A microcontroller, flexible battery and wireless module are attached using conductive adhesives or stitching.
  6. Encapsulate. A waterproof, stretchable polymer is applied to protect the active layers from moisture and mechanical wear.
  7. Calibrate and test. Sensor response is measured against reference instruments, and the garment is washed and stretched to check durability.
  8. Assemble garment. The functional fabric is cut and sewn into the final clothing item, with connectors for charging and data.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Scalable graphene production — methods exist but cost, uniformity and volume must improve for mass-market textiles.
  • PARTIAL Flexible, washable batteries — some flexible batteries exist, but energy density and survival through washing cycles remain limited.
  • PARTIAL Conductive textile integration — conductive yarns and coatings are used in niche products, but graphene integration is still being refined.
  • EXISTS Wireless health data standards — Bluetooth Low Energy and similar protocols can transmit data, though medical-grade security and privacy need attention.
  • PARTIAL Regulatory approval for medical wearables — some wearable sensors are cleared for clinical use, but graphene-based garments require new validation.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: laboratory prototypes and early commercial products with basic motion or heart-rate sensing — proof that graphene can survive textile processing and daily wear.
  2. Next: pilot production for fitness and remote patient monitoring — real-world data on comfort, washability and sensor accuracy.
  3. Next: commercial launch of medical-grade graphene clothing for continuous vital-sign monitoring — shift from hospital-based to home-based monitoring for chronic conditions.
  4. Longer term: mainstream adoption with integrated energy harvesting and closed-loop health feedback — personalised preventive healthcare and new fashion-tech applications.
