PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Perovskite-silicon tandems are a type of photovoltaic technology that combines perovskite and silicon layers to create highly efficient solar cells by capturing more of the solar spectrum.

2Problem It Solves

Improving the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of solar panels by utilizing perovskite materials that can capture more of the solar spectrum than traditional silicon alone.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

The perovskite layer, with its wide bandgap, captures high-energy blue photons, while the underlying silicon layer handles lower-energy red photons. This stacking allows for a broader range of light absorption compared to single-layer silicon cells.

5Materials Used
  • Perovskites
  • Silicon
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The manufacturing process involves depositing thin layers of perovskite on top of a silicon substrate, typically through solution-based methods like spin coating or inkjet printing. This allows for potentially lower-cost production compared to traditional crystalline silicon processes.

7Build Process

First, the silicon wafer is prepared and cleaned. Perovskite precursor solutions are then applied using techniques such as spin coating or slot-die coating. The perovskite film is annealed to form the desired crystal structure before additional layers of perovskite can be deposited for tandem cells.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Partly there

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Silicon bottom cell — absorbs infrared and red light that passes through the perovskite, and provides the mechanical and electrical base. Key parts: n-type silicon wafer (often TOPCon or heterojunction), textured front surface, rear passivation and contact.
  • Perovskite top cell — a wide-bandgap absorber, tuned to around 1.6–1.7 eV, that harvests the blue and green photons silicon cannot use well. Key parts: perovskite absorber layer (lead halide with mixed organic cations), additives and passivators, capping layer.
  • Charge transport and interconnect — selectively extracts electrons and holes and, in a two-terminal stack, joins the two cells electrically. Key parts: hole transport layer, electron transport layer, recombination junction or transparent interconnect.
  • Contacts and light management — collects current across the whole cell while letting light through to the layers below. Key parts: transparent conductive oxide (indium tin oxide or similar), metal grid fingers or busbars, antireflection and barrier coatings.
  • Encapsulation — keeps out moisture and oxygen and filters ultraviolet light, which are what destroy a perovskite layer. Key parts: front glass and rear glass or backsheet, edge seal, UV-stable encapsulant.
  • Coating and lamination line — turns the stack into a module at speed and without damage. Key parts: slot-die or thermal evaporation tools, low-temperature sputterer, laminator.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Make the silicon bottom cell. Wafers are textured, doped and passivated into finished high-efficiency silicon cells, then their front surface is flattened or re-textured so a thin perovskite layer can coat it evenly.
  2. Deposit the interconnect and transport layers. Thin layers that select charge carriers and join the two sub-cells are deposited on the silicon, often by sputtering or atomic layer deposition.
  3. Coat the perovskite. A precursor ink of lead halide salts and organic cations is spread over the cell — by spin coating in the lab, or slot-die coating or hybrid evaporation on a production line — then dried and annealed so it crystallises into a uniform film about half a micrometre thick.
  4. Add the top transport and electrode layers. The electron or hole transport layer is coated, then a transparent conductive oxide is sputtered gently enough not to damage the perovskite beneath it.
  5. Metallise and isolate. Metal fingers and busbars are printed or plated to carry current out, and the cell edges are cleaned and isolated.
  6. Encapsulate and laminate. The stack is sealed between glass panes with an edge seal and a UV-blocking encapsulant — the step that decides whether the module survives outdoors.
  7. Flash-test and light-soak. Finished modules are measured under standard test conditions, then exposed to light and heat for days to screen out cells that degrade, and binned by output.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS High-efficiency silicon cells — the tandem adds to an already good bottom cell; on a mediocre one the gain is smaller.
  • PARTIAL Scalable perovskite deposition — spin coating works in the lab but wastes ink and is too slow for factories; slot-die and evaporation are being proven on pilot lines.
  • PARTIAL Perovskite stability and encapsulation — perovskites degrade with moisture, heat, light and ultraviolet, and a commercial module must last 25 years, not months.
  • PARTIAL Low-damage transparent electrode deposition — sputtering the top electrode is an energetic process that can ruin the perovskite underneath.
  • NOT YET Lead handling and recycling infrastructure — most high-performing perovskites contain lead, so end-of-life recovery and safe manufacturing must be built in.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: pilot production of two-terminal tandem modules on full-size wafers — real field and factory data on yield, stability and cost.
  2. Next: tandem modules in utility-scale demonstration plants alongside silicon panels — evidence that they out-produce standard modules over years, not weeks.
  3. Next: mechanically stacked four-terminal tandems and first commercial-scale lines — easier integration with existing silicon lines, since each sub-cell is made and tested separately.
  4. Longer term: all-perovskite tandems and triple junctions — cells well beyond silicon's theoretical ceiling, with lighter, flexible modules.

18Glossary
Perovskite
A class of materials with a specific crystal structure that can be used in solar cells due to their ability to absorb and convert light efficiently.
Tandem Cells
Photovoltaic cells composed of multiple layers, each optimized for different parts of the solar spectrum. In perovskite-silicon tandems, this means combining silicon with a perovskite layer to capture more light.
