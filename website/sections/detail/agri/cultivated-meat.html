PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Cultivated meat, also known as lab-grown or cell-based meat, is a form of alternative protein produced by growing animal cells in bioreactors instead of raising and slaughtering animals. These cells are typically derived from a small sample of muscle tissue and grown in nutrient-rich media on scaffolds that mimic the structure of natural tissues.

2Problem It Solves

Cultivated meat addresses environmental concerns related to traditional animal agriculture, such as land use, water consumption, greenhouse gas emissions, and deforestation. It also offers potential solutions for reducing the risk of zoonotic diseases and improving animal welfare by eliminating the need for livestock farming and slaughter.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

Animal cells are isolated from donor animals, usually through a biopsy process. These cells are then placed in bioreactors where they are cultured in a nutrient medium containing essential nutrients, growth factors, and other components necessary for cell proliferation and differentiation. The cells grow and multiply on scaffolds that provide structural support to form muscle tissue. This process is regulated by various biological processes such as pH control, temperature management, and the addition of growth factors. Once sufficient biomass has been achieved, the cultured meat is harvested, processed into desired forms (e.g., ground meat or sliced products), and can be used in a variety of food applications.

6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The manufacturing process involves several key steps: cell isolation, bioreactor cultivation, tissue engineering, and product formation. Each step requires precise control over environmental conditions and nutrient supply to ensure optimal growth and differentiation of cells into muscle tissues.

7Build Process

Pilot facilities for cultivated meat typically involve setting up and maintaining bioreactors capable of scaling the production process from small-scale experiments to larger pilot runs. This includes developing robust cell lines, optimizing culture media formulations, and refining bioprocess parameters such as oxygenation, pH control, and temperature management.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Cell line — the living factory — animal cells that multiply and produce muscle and fat tissue. Key parts: primary muscle stem cells from a biopsy, induced pluripotent stem cells, immortalised cell lines for sustained growth.
  • Culture media — liquid nutrient broth that feeds the cells and signals them to grow. Key parts: water, salts, sugars and amino acids, vitamins and trace elements, growth factors and hormones (now moving to animal-free formulations).
  • Bioreactor — a controlled vessel where cells grow in large numbers. Key parts: stirred-tank or airlift vessel, impeller and aeration system, sensors for temperature, pH, oxygen and nutrients, perfusion system to remove waste and add fresh media.
  • Scaffold — gives three-dimensional structure so cells form textured meat rather than a loose paste. Key parts: edible plant-derived fibres, collagen or other proteins, biodegradable synthetic polymers.
  • Harvest and forming — separates cells from media and shapes them into food products. Key parts: centrifuge or filtration, washing and concentration, moulding, extrusion or binding equipment.
  • Quality and safety control — ensures the product is sterile, consistent and safe to eat. Key parts: sterility and mycoplasma tests, nutrient and metabolite assays, regulatory documentation.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Take a cell sample. A small biopsy is taken from a live animal under anaesthesia; muscle stem cells or other suitable cells are isolated.
  2. Establish and bank the cells. The cells are grown in small flasks, selected for rapid growth, and frozen in vials as a working cell bank.
  3. Prepare the growth media. A precise mixture of water, nutrients, salts and growth factors is blended and sterilised; modern formulations avoid animal serum.
  4. Expand in seed trains. Cells are progressively transferred into larger vessels, from millilitres to hundreds of litres, to build up biomass.
  5. Culture in production bioreactors. The cells are grown in pilot-scale stirred-tank bioreactors, either free-floating or attached to edible scaffolds, with temperature, pH and oxygen tightly controlled.
  6. Harvest the cells. The broth is drained; cells are separated from the liquid by centrifugation or filtration, then washed.
  7. Form the product. The harvested cells are mixed with binders, fats or plant proteins and shaped into nuggets, patties or structured pieces.
  8. Package and test. Products are sealed, chilled or frozen, and samples are tested for safety and consistency before release.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Animal-free cell culture media — traditional media uses fetal bovine serum, which is expensive, variable and ethically problematic; serum-free alternatives are being scaled up.
  • PARTIAL Large-scale bioreactors for animal cells — pilot plants exist in the thousands of litres, but tens of thousands of litres are needed for cost parity with conventional meat.
  • PARTIAL Edible scaffolds for texture — most current products are minced or formed; whole cuts require scaffolds that support aligned muscle fibres.
  • PARTIAL Regulatory approval frameworks — Singapore approved sales in 2020 and the United States granted its first approvals in 2023, but most countries still lack a clear pathway.
  • NOT YET Cost reduction to parity — cultivated meat remains far more expensive per kilogram than conventional meat, mainly due to media and bioreactor costs.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: pilot plants producing small volumes; first regulatory approvals in Singapore and the United States — proof that cultivated meat can be made and sold legally.
  2. Next: hybrid products that blend cultivated fat or cells with plant protein — lower cost and familiar formats, helping build consumer acceptance.
  3. Next: bioreactors in the tens of thousands of litres and serum-free media at scale — cost per kilogram falling toward parity with conventional meat.
  4. Longer term: structured whole cuts and large-scale plants — cultivated meat as a mainstream protein with far lower land and water use.

18Glossary
Bioreactor
A controlled environment for growing cells or microorganisms, often used in biotechnology processes such as cultivated meat production.
Nutrient Media
A liquid or semi-solid substance containing essential nutrients and growth factors necessary for the survival and proliferation of cultured cells.
Scaffolds
Three-dimensional structures that provide support and guidance to growing cells during tissue engineering processes, helping to form organized tissues like muscle.
Growth Factors
Proteins or other molecules that stimulate cell proliferation and differentiation, essential for the development of cultured meat from isolated cells.
