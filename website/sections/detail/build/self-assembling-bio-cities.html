PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Not yet possible

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Mycelium scaffold — fungal threads grow through crop waste or sawdust and bind it into a solid, lightweight matrix. Key parts: fungal strain selection, lignocellulosic substrate, mould or formwork.
  • Biomineralisation system — microbes or enzymes precipitate calcium carbonate inside the matrix, hardening it and locking away carbon. Key parts: ureolytic or carbonic-anhydrase bacteria, calcium-rich feed solution, percolation channels.
  • Protective skin — stops water ingress and UV damage, and keeps the fungus from resuming growth or rotting later. Key parts: mineralised outer layer, water-repellent biopolymer coating.
  • Nutrient and water network — channels that feed and hydrate a structure that is still living in places. Key parts: capillary channels, drip or mist lines, nutrient reservoir.
  • Sensing and control layer — measures moisture, strain and biological contamination so the material can be managed. Key parts: embedded humidity and strain sensors, wireless data link, control software.
  • Repair and reprocessing loop — re-inoculates damaged zones and, at end of life, breaks material down for reuse. Key parts: spore or culture supply, shredding and re-forming equipment.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Design the form and the biology. The intended shape is modelled computationally, then fungal strains and mineralising microbes are chosen to suit the local climate and feedstock.
  2. Prepare the substrate. Agricultural waste or sawdust is sterilised, wetted and packed into moulds or fabric formworks.
  3. Grow the hyphal network. Inoculated substrate is kept warm and humid for one to several weeks while hyphae colonise and bind every particle.
  4. Harden by biomineralisation. A calcium-rich solution carrying bacteria or enzymes is percolated through the matrix, precipitating calcite crystals between the fibres over days to weeks.
  5. Seal the surface. The outer skin is mineralised or coated so the material resists rain, frost and further fungal growth.
  6. Assemble modules on site. Blocks and panels are transported, stacked or joined, and the joints are re-inoculated so adjacent pieces fuse into one body.
  7. Activate living functions. Capillary water and nutrient lines, sensors and control electronics are threaded through the structure.
  8. Commission and maintain. Moisture, strain and contamination are monitored, and cracks or eroded zones are re-grown with fresh culture rather than replaced.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Mycelium composite manufacturing — grown packaging and acoustic panels are sold today, but structural load-bearing grades are not.
  • PARTIAL Microbially induced carbonate precipitation — used in trials for soil stabilisation, dust suppression and concrete crack healing, not yet at building scale.
  • PARTIAL Engineered living materials — genetically programmed materials that sense, repair or change shape exist in laboratories only.
  • NOT YET Durability, fire and moisture data for bio-based structure — no long-term evidence exists that a grown wall survives decades of weather, insects and fire.
  • NOT YET Standards and certification for living building materials — building codes assume inert materials with predictable, testable properties.
  • PARTIAL Verifiable carbon storage — the carbon locked into the material must be shown to stay locked for the building's life.
12Theoretical Path to Reality
  1. Now: non-structural mycelium panels and biocemented ground, plus coral-analogue reef restoration — real production experience and cost data for grown materials.
  2. Next: load-light grown blocks and wall systems in low-rise buildings — first certified structural uses and long-term durability records.
  3. Next: self-healing modular structures with embedded sensors — buildings that repair their own cracks, cutting maintenance.
  4. Longer term: larger grown districts using local waste as feedstock — construction that stores carbon rather than emitting it, if durability and codes are solved.
