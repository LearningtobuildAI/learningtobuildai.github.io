PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

CO2-Mineralized Concrete is a type of cementitious material where carbon dioxide (CO2) is chemically bound during its production and curing process, forming stable calcium carbonate minerals within the concrete structure.

2Problem It Solves

This technology addresses the issue of CO2 emissions from the construction sector by permanently sequestering CO2 during the production and curing process of concrete. It also reduces reliance on traditional cement, which has a significant carbon footprint.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

The CO2 is injected into the concrete mix in a liquid or supercritical state. As the concrete cures, the CO2 reacts with the cementitious materials to form calcium carbonate (CaCO3) minerals, effectively storing the CO2 within the concrete's microstructure over time.

5Materials Used
  • calcium carbonate
  • cementitious materials
  • CO2
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The manufacturing process involves mixing conventional concrete materials with captured CO2 before pouring and setting the mixture into forms or molds.

7Build Process

After the concrete sets, it undergoes a curing process where the CO2 is chemically bound to form calcium carbonate minerals. This process can take several days to weeks depending on the environmental conditions.

8Energy Requirements

Field units draw low hundreds of watts; fabrication is energy-intensive due to vacuum baking. The overall energy intensity can be reduced through process optimization and the use of renewable energy sources.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Binder — the reactive cement phases that will combine with CO2 to form solid carbonate. Key parts: Portland or blended clinker, supplementary cementitious materials such as slag or fly ash, limestone filler, gypsum.
  • CO2 supply and dosing — delivers purified, liquefied CO2 into the mixer at a controlled rate. Key parts: tank or on-site liquefaction, mass-flow meter, injection manifold or lance, control unit.
  • Batching and mixing — where aggregate, binder, water, admixtures and CO2 are combined. Key parts: aggregate hoppers, cement silo, mixer drum, admixture dosing pumps.
  • Curing system — either normal moist curing of ready-mix, or a CO2-rich chamber for precast units. Key parts: sealed curing chamber (precast), CO2 atmosphere and humidity control, temperature control.
  • Mineralization chemistry — dissolved CO2 forms carbonate ions that precipitate as calcium carbonate inside the pore structure. Key parts: calcium ions from cement phases, dissolved CO2 (carbonic acid), nucleation sites on binder grains.
  • Testing and accounting — proves the concrete still meets strength and durability specs, and quantifies how much CO2 is locked in. Key parts: compressive strength testing, thermogravimetric or similar carbonate analysis, chain-of-custody records for the CO2.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Capture the CO2. A stream of high-purity CO2 is separated at an industrial plant — often an ethanol, ammonia or gas-processing facility, where the gas is already concentrated. Cement kiln flue gas is dilute and needs more processing.
  2. Purify and deliver. The gas is dried, compressed or liquefied, and shipped by tanker or pipeline to the concrete plant.
  3. Batch the mix. Sand, crushed rock, cement, water, admixtures and any supplementary materials are weighed and loaded into the mixer using an established mix design.
  4. Inject and mix. A metered dose of CO2 is fed into the drum during mixing. It dissolves, reacts with calcium in the binder, and precipitates fine calcium carbonate particles that fill pores and add early strength.
  5. Place the concrete. The mixture is poured, pumped or cast into forms while still workable — the reaction must be tuned so it does not stiffen the mix too early.
  6. Cure. Ready-mix cures conventionally while the mineralized carbonate stays locked in the matrix. Precast blocks, pavers and pipes can instead cure in a sealed chamber flooded with CO2, which reacts across the whole surface.
  7. Test and release. Cylinders are crushed to confirm strength and samples are analysed for carbonate content, so the producer can certify both performance and stored CO2.
  8. Operate and account. The concrete goes into pavements, foundations, walls and pipes, where the carbonate remains stable for the life of the structure — and even after demolition, if the rubble is not recarbonated away.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Industrial CO2 capture — the feedstock has to come from somewhere; today it comes mainly from already-concentrated industrial streams.
  • EXISTS CO2 purification, liquefaction and transport — concrete plants need consistent, dry, metered CO2 rather than raw flue gas.
  • EXISTS Digital batching-plant controls — dosing must be precise; too little does nothing and too much harms workability.
  • PARTIAL Standards and verification for CO2 storage in concrete — buyers and regulators need agreed methods to count and audit the carbon that is genuinely mineralized.
  • PARTIAL Low-clinker and alternative binders — stored CO2 is only a small fraction of concrete's footprint — the far bigger gain is cutting the cement content it is blended into.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: CO2 injection during mixing at commercial ready-mix plants, and CO2 curing of precast blocks and pavers — a modest but real emissions cut per cubic metre plus a strength benefit, and a business case that needs no subsidy for the gas itself.
  2. Next: carbonation of recycled concrete aggregate and crushed demolition waste — a way to store CO2 in material that would otherwise be landfilled, and to improve recycled aggregate quality.
  3. Next: combination with low-clinker binders and alternative cements — much larger emission reductions, with mineralization handling the residual carbon that cannot be designed out.
  4. Longer term: designed-in carbonation of the whole concrete life cycle, including end-of-life rubble — concrete acting as a durable, verifiable carbon sink rather than only a source.

18Glossary
supercritical CO2
A state of carbon dioxide where it exists between its liquid and gas phases, allowing for efficient injection into concrete mixes.
cementitious materials
Materials that set and harden by reacting with water, including cement and other binders used in concrete production.
