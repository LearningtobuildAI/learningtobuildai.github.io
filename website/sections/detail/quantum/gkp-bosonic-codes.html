PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill codes encode a logical qubit in a periodic grid of oscillator states, correcting both bit and phase flips within a single bosonic mode and enabling hardware-efficient error correction.

4How It Works

A microwave cavity is driven into a lattice of squeezed states; small displacement errors shift the lattice and are detected and corrected by measuring stabiliser displacements without collapsing the encoded logical information.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Oscillator (bosonic mode) — stores the logical qubit as a standing pattern of states in phase space rather than in a two-level system. Key parts: high-Q microwave cavity or superconducting resonator, or a trapped-ion motional mode, low-loss materials and shielding.
  • Ancilla control qubit — supplies the nonlinearity needed to create, squeeze and read the grid states. Key parts: transmon qubit with a Josephson junction, couplers to the oscillator, readout resonator and amplifier.
  • Squeezing and displacement drives — shape the lattice of states and control the spacing between its peaks. Key parts: phase-locked microwave sources, arbitrary waveform generators, parametric amplifier for squeezing, optical squeezers (photonic route).
  • Stabiliser measurement and feedback — detects small displacements without collapsing the encoded logical information. Key parts: quantum-limited amplifiers, fast digitisers and FPGA feedback, conditional displacement pulses, Pauli-frame tracking software.
  • Cryogenics and shielding — keeps the oscillator cold, quiet and free of stray fields. Key parts: dilution refrigerator at roughly 10 millikelvin, magnetic and infrared shielding, microwave filters.
  • Classical decoder — decides whether a displacement drifted past the halfway point between lattice peaks. Key parts: real-time decoder on FPGA or GPU, outer-code decoder.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Fabricate the oscillator. Machine and polish a niobium or aluminium cavity, or pattern a superconducting resonator on a low-loss substrate, until it holds a microwave photon for milliseconds.
  2. Build the ancilla qubit. Fabricate a transmon with a Josephson junction, wire up its readout, and measure how long it holds its state.
  3. Assemble and cool down. Mount oscillator and qubit in a dilution refrigerator at about 10 millikelvin, shield them from magnetic fields, and install filtered microwave lines.
  4. Calibrate the coupling. Tune the dispersive interaction between qubit and oscillator and verify the drives that will squeeze the oscillator's quantum noise.
  5. Engineer the grid state. Drive the oscillator while repeatedly measuring the qubit so it settles into a superposition of squeezed states arranged on a square lattice, confirmed as a grid of peaks in the Wigner function.
  6. Verify with tomography. Displace the state and measure it to map the lattice, and extract how narrow the peaks are relative to their spacing — the squeezing that sets the code's strength.
  7. Run correction cycles. Repeatedly measure the displacement stabilisers; a photon-loss event shifts the lattice by less than half a cell and is detected, then cancelled by a conditional displacement or tracked in software as a frame update, without ever reading the logical qubit.
  8. Concatenate and scale. Wrap the oscillator code inside an outer qubit code and inject non-Clifford magic states so the encoded qubit can be assembled into a processor.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS High-coherence oscillators — the logical qubit can only be as good as the mode storing it; microwave cavities with quality factors in the millions are routine.
  • EXISTS Quantum-limited amplifiers — stabiliser measurements must not inject more noise than the code can absorb.
  • PARTIAL High-fidelity ancilla qubits and gates — control errors during preparation and readout set a floor on how well errors can be corrected.
  • PARTIAL Squeezing of the order of ten decibels — fault-tolerant operation needs lattice peaks narrow compared with their spacing, and laboratory squeezing still falls short in the systems used.
  • PARTIAL Real-time feedback and fast decoding — corrections must land before the state decoheres, so latency in the measurement chain matters.
  • PARTIAL Universal (non-Clifford) gate resource — full computation needs magic states or equivalent operations that bosonic codes do not supply for free.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: the code, proposed by Gottesman, Kitaev and Preskill in 2001, has been engineered and error-corrected at the single-mode level in superconducting cavities and trapped-ion oscillators — proof that a continuum degree of freedom can hold a protected qubit.
  2. Next: higher squeezing and longer-lived logical qubits, alongside companion bosonic encodings such as cat codes — hardware-efficient logical qubits that beat any physical qubit with far fewer components.
  3. Next: concatenation with an outer code and magic-state distillation — a genuinely fault-tolerant logical qubit and a route to universal computation.
  4. Longer term: modules of many such qubits, including photonic machines built from squeezed light — scalable quantum processors for simulation, chemistry and cryptography.
