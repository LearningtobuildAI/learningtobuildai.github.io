9Engineering Breakdown
  • Abiotic pathway catalogue — lists every known non-biological way to make a candidate gas, so a detection is never interpreted in isolation. Key parts: stellar and photochemical sources, geological and volcanic sources, impact and escape processes.
  • Photochemical and climate models — simulate the candidate planet's atmosphere to see whether the observed gases can coexist without life. Key parts: photochemical kinetics, radiative transfer, atmospheric escape and climate codes.
  • Retrieval and instrument model — turns a measured spectrum into gas abundances with honest error bars. Key parts: atmospheric retrieval code, spectral line databases, telescope noise and systematics model.
  • Planetary context dataset — supplies the star, orbit and environment that determine which explanations are plausible. Key parts: stellar ultraviolet and X-ray flux, planet mass and radius, orbital and irradiation history.
  • Assessment logic — the decision rules that convert model comparisons into a stated confidence level. Key parts: hypothesis comparison or scoring scheme, explicit uncertainty reporting, falsification criteria.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Characterise star and planet. Measure the host star's brightness and ultraviolet output and the planet's mass, radius and orbit; these set which chemistry is possible.
  2. Retrieve the spectrum. Fit the observed transmission or emission spectrum to derive abundances of gases such as oxygen, methane, carbon dioxide and water, with uncertainties.
  3. Enumerate abiotic sources. Working through the catalogue, list every non-biological route to each detected gas — for example oxygen from water vapour broken apart by ultraviolet light followed by hydrogen escape.
  4. Run the models. Feed each plausible scenario into photochemical and climate models and check whether it reproduces the observation within the error bars.
  5. Compare hypotheses. Score or rank the biological and abiotic explanations against the same data rather than testing life alone.
  6. Report the surviving gap. State what remains unexplained after abiotic options are exhausted, with uncertainty, instead of presenting the raw detection as a life claim.
  7. Set falsification tests. Specify which follow-up observation — another gas, a different wavelength band, a time-variation — would break the preferred explanation.
  8. Reassess as data improve. Re-run the framework whenever better spectra, stellar data or new abiotic pathways appear, and revise the confidence accordingly.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Exoplanet atmospheric spectroscopy — water and carbon dioxide have been measured in hot giant planets since 2022, but thin atmospheres around small rocky worlds remain at the edge of capability.
  • EXISTS Validated photochemical and climate models — codes tested against Venus, Earth and Titan underpin the abiotic baselines; extending them to unfamiliar planets is the weak point.
  • EXISTS Laboratory spectral line data — measured absorption line lists are needed to identify gases in a spectrum at all.
  • PARTIAL Knowledge of abiotic gas production — new non-biological pathways keep being identified, so any catalogue is provisional.
  • PARTIAL Telescopes able to see several gases at once — a single gas means little; a confident assessment needs simultaneous detection of a combination that ordinary chemistry cannot sustain.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: framework applied to solar-system planets, Archean Earth analogues and simulated exoplanet spectra, and used to interpret current telescope data — discipline of publishing explanatory gaps rather than bare detections.
  2. Next: application to the first rocky-planet atmospheres resolved with current and under-construction telescopes — real test cases, and pressure to standardise the scoring and uncertainty conventions.
  3. Next: reflected-light spectra of temperate Earth-sized planets from a dedicated direct-imaging space telescope — multiple gases measured together, where the framework's hypothesis comparison becomes decisive.
  4. Longer term: observational networks that track seasonal and long-term variability, plus agnostic signatures for unfamiliar biochemistry — biosignature claims that rest on repeated, independent lines of evidence rather than one spectrum.
