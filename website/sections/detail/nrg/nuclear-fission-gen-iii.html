1Definition

Nuclear Fission Gen III refers to modern light-water reactors designed for efficient and safe operation. These reactors utilize enriched uranium fuel in a controlled chain reaction to produce heat, which is then used to generate steam that drives turbines.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

Enriched uranium fuel rods are arranged in the reactor core, where neutrons from the fission of uranium atoms sustain a controlled nuclear chain reaction. The heat generated by this process boils water into high-pressure steam. This steam is directed through pipes to a turbine, which spins and generates electricity. Passive safety systems are designed to ensure that the reactor remains stable even in extreme conditions.

Reliable and consistent electricity supply available at all times, regardless of weather conditions or time of day, essential for stable grid operation.
