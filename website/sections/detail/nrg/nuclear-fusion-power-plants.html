PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Nuclear fusion power plants are facilities designed to produce electricity by replicating the process that powers the sun. In these reactors, atomic nuclei combine under extreme temperatures and pressures to release a significant amount of energy.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

Fusion reactions can be achieved through two primary methods: magnetic confinement (e.g., tokamaks) and inertial confinement (e.g., laser fusion). In magnetic confinement, a plasma is contained in a toroidal chamber using powerful magnetic fields. Inertial confinement uses intense lasers or particle beams to compress fuel pellets, causing them to fuse.

6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The manufacturing process involves constructing highly specialized facilities capable of maintaining conditions necessary for fusion reactions. This includes precise control systems, advanced materials, and complex engineering challenges related to heat management and plasma stability.

7Build Process

Construction begins with site selection and environmental impact assessments. The reactor design is then developed in collaboration with international partners before moving into the fabrication phase where components are manufactured according to stringent specifications. Testing and commissioning follow to ensure safety and performance standards are met.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Not yet possible

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Confinement core — the machine that holds and heats the fusing plasma — a magnetic torus, a stellarator, or a laser-driven target chamber. Key parts: superconducting coils or laser/target system, plasma heating and current drive, vacuum pumping.
  • Blanket and heat-transfer loop — a lithium-bearing wall that absorbs the fusion neutrons, breeds tritium and carries the captured heat away. Key parts: lithium ceramic or molten-salt breeder, coolant channels (helium, water or molten salt), heat exchangers.
  • Tritium plant — recovers bred tritium, separates it from exhaust gas and returns it to the fuel injectors. Key parts: cryogenic distillation or permeation separators, tritium storage beds, deuterium supply from ordinary water.
  • Exhaust and divertor — strips helium 'ash' and heat from the plasma edge before it reaches the walls. Key parts: tungsten target plates, high-heat-flux cooling, pumping.
  • Shielding, vacuum and containment — protects equipment and people from neutrons and keeps the plasma chamber at near-vacuum. Key parts: neutron and gamma shielding, bioshield, cryostat, tritium leak containment.
  • Balance of plant — turns blanket heat into grid electricity, like a conventional thermal station. Key parts: steam generator or supercritical CO2 cycle, turbine and generator, transformer and switchyard.
  • Remote maintenance — handles components that become radioactive through neutron bombardment and cannot be touched. Key parts: robotic arms and cranes, radiation-hardened cameras and tools.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Qualify the materials. Candidate steels and tungsten alloys are irradiated in test reactors and ion beams for years to prove they survive a plant lifetime of neutron damage.
  2. Build the confinement machine. Magnets are wound from superconducting cable or a target chamber is assembled, then the vacuum vessel is welded around them to millimetre tolerances.
  3. Install the first wall and blanket. Breeder modules and plasma-facing tiles are fitted inside the vessel, with coolant plumbing for every module.
  4. Add shielding and the tritium plant. Bioshield concrete or steel goes up around the machine, and the tritium processing building is connected as a sealed, monitored system.
  5. Connect the balance of plant. Heat exchangers, turbine hall and switchyard are tied to the blanket coolant loop and commissioned on conventional heat sources.
  6. Commission with hydrogen. The plant runs first on hydrogen and deuterium only, learning plasma control and machine behaviour without radioactive fuel.
  7. Introduce deuterium–tritium fuel. Tritium is added, neutron output rises, the blanket starts breeding, and the tritium inventory must balance or the plant cannot keep running.
  8. First electricity to the grid. Heat from the blanket drives the turbine and the plant exports power — the step no fusion device has yet taken.
  9. Decommissioning and waste route. Activated components must be stored and eventually recycled or disposed of under nuclear regulations.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL High-field superconducting magnets or high-gain inertial targets — they set the size, cost and achievable plasma performance of the whole plant.
  • PARTIAL Sustained net energy gain — a single laser shot reached net gain in 2022, but no device has produced sustained fusion power with a practical fuel cycle.
  • NOT YET Neutron-tolerant structural materials — fusion neutrons damage materials far faster than fission neutrons, and no qualified alloy exists for a plant's full service life.
  • NOT YET Tritium breeding and handling at scale — a plant must breed its own tritium from lithium and hold a large, well-contained inventory.
  • PARTIAL Remote handling for activated parts — blanket and divertor modules will need replacement every few years by robots, because radiation rules out human access.
  • NOT YET Fusion-specific licensing and fuel accounting — regulators have no established framework for tritium inventories, activated waste or plant siting.
12Theoretical Path to Reality
  1. Now: ITER assembly in France; laser ignition demonstrated at a US national facility in 2022; JET's deuterium–tritium record before it shut down in 2023 — physics data at power-plant scale and lessons for the next generation of machines.
  2. Next: pilot plants aiming to generate net electricity, built by national programmes and privately funded ventures — proof that heat can be extracted and converted to power reliably.
  3. Next: demonstration plants with a closed tritium fuel cycle and replaceable blanket modules — self-sufficient fuel, realistic maintenance schedules and cost data for utilities.
  4. Longer term: first commercial fleets, supported by lithium and tritium supply chains and a licensing regime — firm, low-carbon baseload power — provided materials, fuel breeding and remote maintenance all perform as designed.

18Glossary
Plasma
A state of matter where atoms are ionized, resulting in a mixture of free electrons and positively charged ions.
Tokamak
A toroidal-shaped device used for magnetic confinement fusion to contain plasma within a doughnut-shaped vacuum chamber.
Inertial Confinement Fusion (ICF)
A method of achieving nuclear fusion by rapidly compressing and heating fuel targets using high-intensity lasers or particle beams.
