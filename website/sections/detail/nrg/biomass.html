1Definition

Biomass Power refers to the process of converting organic materials such as wood, crop residues, and energy crops into electricity and heat through combustion or gasification.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

Organic matter is combusted or gasified in a boiler. The resulting heat raises steam that drives a turbine to generate electricity. The carbon from this process can be considered cyclically within the context of regrowth cycles.

5Materials Used
  • wood
  • crop residues
  • energy crops
6Manufacturing / Creation Process

The manufacturing process involves sourcing biomass feedstocks, constructing or retrofitting power plants, and integrating necessary equipment such as boilers and turbines.

7Build Process

Construction typically includes site preparation, installation of the boiler system, integration with turbine technology, and connection to the grid. Retrofitting existing facilities can reduce costs but requires careful assessment of structural compatibility.

PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Available today

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Fuel supply and preparation — delivers a consistent, dry feedstock to the boiler. Key parts: wood chips, pellets or agricultural residues, chipping and drying equipment, covered storage, conveyors and feed systems.
  • Combustion or gasification unit — converts biomass into hot gas or steam. Key parts: boiler or gasifier, grate or fluidised bed, air fans and preheaters, refractory lining.
  • Steam cycle and turbine — turns heat into electricity and useful heat. Key parts: steam drum and superheater, steam turbine, generator, condenser and cooling system.
  • Flue gas cleaning — removes pollutants before release. Key parts: cyclone or multicyclone, baghouse or electrostatic precipitator, scrubber, selective catalytic reduction (SCR) for NOx.
  • Ash handling and byproduct management — collects ash for disposal or reuse. Key parts: ash hoppers, conveyors, ash storage silos, bottom ash handling.
  • Heat recovery and district heating (optional) — captures waste heat for local use. Key parts: heat exchangers, district heating network, thermal store.
10How It's Made — Step by Step
  1. Secure sustainable feedstock. Contract with suppliers of wood residues, energy crops or agricultural waste, ensuring regrowth or waste sourcing keeps the fuel renewable.
  2. Site and permit. Choose a site near feedstock and grid connections; obtain air-quality, water and planning permits.
  3. Build fuel handling and storage. Construct covered storage, dryers and conveyors to keep fuel dry and flowing.
  4. Fabricate and erect the boiler or gasifier. Assemble a large pressure vessel with refractory lining and air supply; for gasification, add a reactor and gas cleaning.
  5. Install the steam turbine and generator. Place the turbine and generator, align them, and connect to the steam system and condenser.
  6. Install emissions controls. Fit particulate filters, acid-gas scrubbers and NOx reduction equipment to meet air-quality limits.
  7. Connect to grid and heat network. Build a substation and transformers for electricity export; lay pipes if supplying district heating.
  8. Commission and test. Start the plant on fuel, tune combustion, and verify emissions and output.
  9. Operate and maintain. Manage fuel supply, remove ash, and service the turbine and emissions systems over decades.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Sustainable biomass supply — The technology exists, but scaling can compete with food, forests and land use, so truly sustainable volumes are limited.
  • EXISTS Combustion and steam turbine technology — Mature from coal and waste plants; biomass needs adapted fuel handling and corrosion-resistant materials.
  • EXISTS Flue gas cleaning — Proven in other industries, but biomass flue gases have different chemistry (e.g. alkali and chloride).
  • PARTIAL Carbon capture and storage (for BECCS) — Demonstrated at pilot scale on biomass, but not yet widely deployed; it is what would make biomass power carbon-negative.
  • EXISTS Grid connection and dispatchable operation — Standard power plant engineering, but biomass plants must manage fuel logistics to run reliably.
12Where It Goes Next
  1. Now: Biomass power plants providing dispatchable renewable electricity and heat, often converted from coal plants. — Firm low-carbon power that can run when wind and solar are low.
  2. Next: More combined heat and power and district heating schemes, plus higher-efficiency boilers. — Better use of the same fuel for both electricity and local heating.
  3. Next: Biomass with carbon capture and storage (BECCS) at commercial scale. — Negative emissions, valuable for meeting net-zero targets.
  4. Longer term: Advanced gasification and integrated biorefineries making power, heat and fuels together. — Higher efficiency and flexible products from the same biomass.

18Glossary
Biomass
Organic matter derived from plants or plant-derived materials.
Gasification
A process where organic material is converted into a gas through partial combustion in an oxygen-limited environment.
Boiler
A closed vessel designed to hold liquid and transfer heat by means of vaporization and subsequent condensation.
Turbine
A machine that converts the energy of a moving fluid into mechanical energy through the use of a rotor blade assembly.
Dispatchable Renewable Energy
Renewable energy sources that can be controlled and adjusted to meet demand, such as biomass power plants which can ramp up or down generation based on grid needs.
