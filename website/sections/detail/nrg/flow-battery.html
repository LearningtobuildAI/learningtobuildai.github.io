PART 1Executive Overview
1Definition

Flow batteries are electrochemical storage systems that store energy in liquid electrolytes contained within external tanks. The energy can be scaled independently by adjusting the volume of the electrolyte tanks, which allows for long-duration storage.

2Problem It Solves

Flow batteries address the need for long-duration storage that can support grid-scale applications without the limitations on energy density found in solid-state battery technologies.

PART 2Technical & Manufacturing
4How It Works

In a flow battery, two tanks hold different electrolyte solutions. These solutions pass through an electrochemical cell stack where they react to store and release energy. Power output is controlled by the size of the cell stack, while energy capacity is determined by the volume of electrolyte in the tanks.

6Manufacturing / Creation Process

Manufacturing involves creating the electrochemical cell stacks, designing and constructing the liquid electrolyte storage tanks, and integrating these components into a functional system. The process requires precision in material selection and assembly to ensure efficient operation and long lifetimes.

The build process includes component design, fabrication of cell stacks using materials like electrodes and membranes, tank construction, and integration testing to ensure the system operates as intended before deployment.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Electrolyte and tanks — stores the energy as two liquids in separate tanks; doubling the tank volume doubles the stored energy without touching the stack. Key parts: positive electrolyte tank, negative electrolyte tank, vanadium, iron, zinc or organic redox salts dissolved in acid or alkaline solution, level and temperature sensors.
  • Cell stack — converts electrical energy to chemical energy and back; more cells or larger cells mean more power. Key parts: carbon or graphite felt electrodes, bipolar plates with flow channels, ion-exchange membrane between each half-cell, gaskets and end plates, clamping bolts or tie rods.
  • Hydraulic loop — pumps both electrolytes through the stacks at a steady rate. Key parts: pumps, piping and valves, flow meters, drip trays and secondary containment.
  • Power conversion — turns the stack's direct current into grid-compatible alternating current in both directions. Key parts: bidirectional inverter, transformer, DC busbars and contactors, stack voltage monitoring.
  • Thermal and rebalancing system — keeps electrolyte in its working temperature range and corrects the slow migration of active material across the membrane. Key parts: heat exchanger or chiller, rebalancing cell or electrolyte re-mixing loop, ventilation.
  • Controls — runs charge and discharge cycles, tracks state of charge and protects the stack. Key parts: battery management controller, state-of-charge estimator, SCADA link to the grid operator.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Produce the active material. Vanadium pentoxide is reduced and dissolved in sulfuric acid to make electrolyte at roughly 1.5–2 moles of vanadium per litre — often sourced from steel-slag or mining residues. Iron, zinc and organic flow chemistries start from cheaper salts or synthesised molecules.
  2. Cast the membrane. A perfluorosulfonic-acid polymer or a hydrocarbon alternative is cast into a thin film a few tens of micrometres thick. It must let charge-carrying ions through while blocking the active species from mixing.
  3. Make the electrodes and plates. Carbon felt or cloth is heat-treated, and conductive carbon-polymer composite plates are moulded with flow channels that spread electrolyte evenly across the felt.
  4. Stack the cells. Electrode, membrane and plate are repeated tens to over a hundred times, compressed between end plates so each cell sits at about 1.2–1.6 V and the stack totals tens to hundreds of volts.
  5. Build the hydraulic loop. Tanks, pumps, pipework and sensors are assembled, usually inside shipping-container-sized modules that can be trucked to site and bolted together.
  6. Fill and condition. Both tanks are filled with electrolyte and the stack is cycled gently so the electrodes and membrane reach stable performance.
  7. Connect and commission. The inverter is tied to the grid transformer, and charge/discharge cycles are run to verify round-trip efficiency, typically 65–80% for vanadium systems, and to calibrate state of charge.
  8. Operate and service. The system runs for 20 years or more with tens of thousands of cycles; maintenance is mostly pump, sensor and electrolyte top-up work rather than cell replacement.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • EXISTS Ion-exchange membranes — the cell only works if one ion can cross while the two electrolytes stay separate; membrane cost is a large share of system cost.
  • EXISTS Vanadium and other redox-active salts at scale — electrolyte is the single biggest cost item, so supply of vanadium, iron or cheap organic molecules sets the economics.
  • EXISTS Bidirectional grid inverters — the stack produces DC and the grid needs AC in both directions.
  • PARTIAL Cheap organic and iron-based chemistries — they could undercut vanadium, but most are only in pilot or early commercial use.
  • PARTIAL Very low-cost tank and piping manufacturing — long duration is won by buying cheap liquid containment, not expensive electrochemistry.
12Path to Full Scale
  1. Now: vanadium systems of tens to hundreds of megawatts connected to grids, including a 100 MW / 400 MWh installation in Dalian, China, energised in 2022 — multi-hour storage that can be cycled daily for decades without capacity fade.
  2. Next: iron, zinc and organic flow chemistries moving from pilots to commercial production — storage that does not depend on vanadium prices and can be built from abundant materials.
  3. Next: electrolyte leasing and reuse, with the liquid recovered and refurbished at end of life — much lower lifetime cost, since the electrolyte outlives the stack.
  4. Longer term: routines of 12–100 hour storage built alongside wind and solar farms — firm power through multi-day calm or cloudy periods, displacing gas peaking plants.

18Glossary
Flow Battery
A type of electrochemical battery that stores energy in liquid electrolyte tanks, allowing for independent scaling of power and energy capacity.
Electrolyte
The fluid component of a flow battery that contains the ions used to store and release electrical charge through chemical reactions.
Cell Stack
A series of electrochemical cells connected in series or parallel to form a larger power output unit within a flow battery system.
