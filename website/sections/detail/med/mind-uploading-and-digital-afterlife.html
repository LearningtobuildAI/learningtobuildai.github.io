PART 3Engineering & Build Guide
Not yet possible

Written by DeepSeek (deepseek-flash) on 29 Sep 2026. General engineering knowledge, not a manufacturer's procedure. Steps for anything not yet built are a theoretical route, not a plan anyone is executing.

9Engineering Breakdown
  • Preservation and fixation — stabilises the brain's structure so it can be imaged without decay. Key parts: perfusion fixation chemicals, cryoprotectants, cold storage or vitrification.
  • Imaging — captures every neuron, axon and synapse at nanometre resolution. Key parts: serial-section electron microscopes, diamond knife sectioning, automated tape/wafer collection, fluorescence and expansion microscopy for molecular labels.
  • Reconstruction software — turns terabytes of images into a wiring diagram. Key parts: image alignment, machine-learning segmentation of cells and synapses, human proofreading interfaces.
  • Physiological model — gives each reconstructed cell realistic firing behaviour. Key parts: ion-channel models, synapse strength estimates, cell-type classification.
  • Simulation platform — runs the network fast enough to behave in real time. Key parts: parallel compute cluster, neuron and synapse data structures, input/output interfaces.
  • Verification rig — tests whether the simulation behaves like the original person. Key parts: recorded brain activity for comparison, behavioural tests, virtual or robotic embodiment.
10How It Would Be Made — Step by Step
  1. Preserve the tissue. Immediately after death, the brain would be chemically fixed by perfusion so proteins and membranes stay in place, then protected against freezing damage and kept cold.
  2. Cut and image. The brain would be sliced into tens of thousands of ultrathin sections, each imaged in an electron microscope — a task already done for small animals but never for a whole human brain.
  3. Reconstruct the wiring. Software stitches the images into a 3D volume and traces each neuron's branches, labelling the ~10,000 synapses on it. This needs machine learning plus years of human proofreading.
  4. Infer cell properties. Every neuron would be assigned a type and a set of electrical and chemical parameters, inferred from physiology experiments and molecular stains, not from the wiring alone.
  5. Build the simulation. The wiring graph and cell parameters would be loaded onto a supercomputer that steps the network forward in time, from milliseconds to hours.
  6. Calibrate against real brains. The model would be tuned until its activity patterns match recordings from living tissue of the same type.
  7. Give it senses and actions. The simulation would be linked to cameras, microphones and robotic or virtual bodies so it can receive input and act.
  8. Test and interpret. Behaviour and internal reports would be compared with the original person. Crucially, no test can prove that the simulation is conscious rather than a very good copy — that question is philosophical, not experimental.
11Prerequisite Technologies
  • PARTIAL Whole-brain connectome at synaptic resolution — completed for the 302-neuron worm and mapped in part for fruit flies and mouse cortex; a human brain is roughly a million times larger and is not close.
  • PARTIAL Scalable electron microscopy and data infrastructure — imaging a whole human brain would produce petabytes to exabytes of images needing automated handling.
  • PARTIAL Biophysical neuron and network simulation — detailed models of small circuits exist, but whole-brain real-time simulation is far out of reach.
  • PARTIAL Brain preservation that keeps fine structure — aldehyde fixation preserves synapses well for imaging, but whether it preserves the information that makes a person is unproven.
  • NOT YET A defensible theory of what must be copied — without knowing whether consciousness depends on molecular states, electrical patterns or something else, no scanning plan can be shown to be sufficient.
12Theoretical Path to Reality
  1. Now: complete connectomes of small animals and partial maps of mammal cortex — automated reconstruction methods and proof that the approach scales at all.
  2. Next: a whole mouse brain connectome with cell types and synapse strengths — simulations of a mammalian brain that can be compared with real behaviour.
  3. Next: non-destructive or high-fidelity whole-human scanning and preservation — a dataset from which a human emulation could in principle be built.
  4. Longer term: a simulation that passes behavioural tests indistinguishable from the original — intense ethical and legal debate, because a copy's status as the same person would remain unresolved.
  5. Speculative: routine digital afterlives — only if the consciousness question is answered — this stage is currently speculation, not engineering.
